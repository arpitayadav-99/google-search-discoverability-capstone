Google Search Ranking & Discoverability

Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Built on the FlyRank ML Internship dataset.

Public-safe framing: This research identifies observed associations and prioritisation signals. It does not reverse-engineer Google's ranking algorithm and does not claim that refreshing a page will causally improve rankings.

Abstract

Content teams need a repeatable way to prioritise pages for search-performance review. This capstone constructs historical page-level features from the FlyRank daily warehouse and labels future-window decline using a strictly separated time window. A rule-based baseline and multiple machine-learning candidates are compared using validation-only model selection and a held-out later test period. The final model produces a ranked opportunity queue with deterministic reason codes. The results are decision-support evidence about observed search-performance patterns, not causal evidence about Google or the effect of content refreshes.

1 · Introduction / Problem Statement

The decision supported by this project is: which content pages should be prioritised for review or refresh? The goal is to rank opportunities consistently rather than claim that any single signal determines search rankings.

3 · Methodology

Historical → future design

For each cutoff, features use the half-open historical window [cutoff − 90d, cutoff). The label uses the separate future window [cutoff, cutoff + 30d). The cutoff date therefore cannot contribute to both features and labels.

Label

A page is eligible when historical impressions meet the minimum activity threshold. The binary decline label is 1 when future clicks per active day fall by at least 30% relative to historical clicks per active day. Pages without adequate historical activity are excluded from the supervised label.

Features

Features include historical impressions/clicks/sessions, CTR, activity days, average position, and pre-cutoff momentum calculated from two non-overlapping historical sub-windows. Future-window measurements are never model features.

Models and baseline

  • Rule-based prioritisation baseline
  • Logistic Regression
  • Random Forest
  • HistGradientBoosting

PR-AUC is the primary validation selection metric. The test period is not used for model selection.

4 · Validation & Leakage Checks

The final experiment uses chronological cutoff snapshots: earlier cutoffs for training, a later cutoff for validation, and the latest cutoff for final testing. Unit tests verify half-open temporal boundaries, label-column exclusion, scaler isolation and deterministic recommendation logic.

6 · Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Search-performance associations do not establish causality.
  • The model is a prioritisation system, not a model of Google's ranking algorithm.
  • Future-window labels depend on the chosen activity threshold, window lengths and decline threshold.
  • Results may change across warehouse releases and time periods.
